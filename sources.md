# Sources & Copyright Boundary

> **This does not tell you what the universe wants. It helps you think clearly before you act.**

This project is *inspired by* selected decision themes from the Bhagavad Gita. It is careful about copyright. This file explains the boundary.

---

## The core distinction

- **The Bhagavad Gita itself is ancient** and the original Sanskrit text is in the public domain.
- **Modern translations and commentaries are usually under copyright.** A translator's specific English wording, a commentator's interpretation, and edited critical editions are creative works owned by their authors and publishers.

So: the ideas are old and free; most of the *words you'll read in a bookshop edition* are not.

## What this repo does

- Uses **original plain-English wording** throughout.
- Refers to the Gita only at the level of **chapter numbers and broad themes** (see [`gita-reference-map.md`](gita-reference-map.md)).
- Treats those themes purely as **decision heuristics**, not as scripture being taught.

## What this repo deliberately avoids

- It does **not** copy any modern translation.
- It does **not** quote modern translations or commentary.
- It does **not** closely paraphrase any famous translation or commentary.
- It does **not** reproduce verse text, numbered-verse renderings, or a translator's distinctive phrasing.

## Public-domain translations (reference only)

If you want to read the text itself, some translations are old enough to be in the public domain in many jurisdictions. These are mentioned **only as reference points** — this project does not reproduce long passages from them:

- **Kashinath Trimbak Telang (1882)** — an early English translation.
- **Edwin Arnold, *The Song Celestial* (1885)** — a verse translation.

Public-domain status depends on your country's copyright law. Verify locally before reproducing anything.

## If you extend this project

- Keep all Gita references at the **theme/chapter** level.
- Write your own plain-English summaries; do not lift wording from a translation or commentary.
- If you ever want to include actual verse text, use a clearly public-domain source for your jurisdiction, attribute it, and keep excerpts short — or, better, link out instead of reproducing.
- When in doubt, describe the idea in your own words rather than quoting.

## Not legal advice

This page explains the project's self-imposed boundary. It is not legal advice about copyright. If you plan to publish or sell anything that reproduces translation or commentary text, check your jurisdiction's rules or consult a professional.
