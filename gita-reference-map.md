# Gita Reference Map (source-safe)

> **This does not tell you what the universe wants. It helps you think clearly before you act.**

This file explains, in original plain English, where the Right Action Framework draws *inspiration* from. It is not a translation, a summary of any commentary, or a teaching of the Bhagavad Gita. It gives **chapter and theme references only**, so you can see which decision idea is loosely behind each part of the framework.

Important boundaries:

- These are **themes**, described in my own plain words.
- No modern translation or commentary is quoted or closely paraphrased.
- Chapter numbers point you to where a theme broadly lives in the text, nothing more.
- The framework treats these purely as **decision heuristics**, not spiritual instruction.

See [`sources.md`](sources.md) for the copyright reasoning.

## Chapter 2 — clarity, steadiness, self-mastery, equanimity

**Theme in plain English:** Act from a clear, steady mind rather than from grief, panic, or paralysis. Don't let a strong feeling collapse your judgment. Stay reasonably level whether things go well or badly.

**How the framework uses it:**
- Question 2 ("What is loud right now?") — name the emotion so it stops driving silently.
- The "What emotion to watch" and "Final reminder" output lines — keep steadiness through the result.

## Chapter 3 — Karma Yoga: duty, right action, action over avoidance

**Theme in plain English:** Doing your legitimate part is usually better than freezing or opting out. Focus on acting well, not on guaranteeing a particular reward. Avoidance is itself a choice, and often a worse one.

**How the framework uses it:**
- Question 3 ("right responsibility") and Question 4 ("the clearest next action to consider").
- The bias toward a concrete next move to weigh rather than stalling.

## Chapter 6 — discipline of the mind, self-control, inner steadiness

**Theme in plain English:** A trained, settled mind makes better decisions than a reactive one. Self-control is a skill you practise, not a mood you wait for.

**How the framework uses it:**
- "Next 3 grounded steps" — small disciplined actions instead of one dramatic reaction.
- The overall calm, non-impulsive tone.

## Chapter 12 — humility, steadiness, non-hatred, patience, balanced conduct

**Theme in plain English:** Conduct yourself with patience and without hatred, even toward people you're in conflict with. Steadiness and fairness are strengths, not weaknesses.

**How the framework uses it:**
- The lower-conflict-first bias in the Dharma vs Ego Legal Check.
- "What NOT to do" — usually the harsh, contemptuous, or vengeful option.

## Chapter 16 — truthfulness, self-restraint, anger, pride, harshness

**Theme in plain English:** There are constructive inner states (honesty, restraint, fairness) and destructive inner states (anger, pride, harshness, deceit). Decisions get worse when the destructive inner states take the wheel.

**How the framework uses it:**
- Question 2's emotion list (anger, pride, revenge, ego).
- The instruction to flag when you're acting mainly from anger, ego, or revenge.

## Chapter 18 — duty, discernment, responsibility, acting according to one's role and nature

**Theme in plain English:** Discern what is actually yours to do, given your role and situation, and do that — rather than taking on what isn't yours or dodging what is.

**How the framework uses it:**
- Question 3 ("right responsibility") — separating your part from others' parts.
- "What I can control / cannot control" — matching effort to your actual role.

## How a theme becomes a framework step

| Framework element | Inspired by (theme reference only) |
|---|---|
| Name the loud emotion | Ch. 2 (steadiness), Ch. 16 (destructive inner states) |
| Find right responsibility | Ch. 3 (duty), Ch. 18 (discernment of one's role) |
| Choose a grounded next action over avoidance | Ch. 3 (action over inaction) |
| Take small disciplined steps | Ch. 6 (discipline of the mind) |
| Lower-conflict, non-hateful conduct | Ch. 12 (patience, non-hatred) |
| Detach from the outcome | Ch. 2 + Ch. 3 (act well, hold the result loosely) |
