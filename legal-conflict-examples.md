# Legal & Conflict Examples

> **This does not tell you what the universe wants. It helps you think clearly before you act.**

Five conflict decisions run through the Dharma vs Ego Legal Check ([`dharma-vs-ego-legal-check.md`](dharma-vs-ego-legal-check.md)).

These illustrate the *shape* of clear thinking. They are **not legal advice**, they do **not predict any outcome**, and they do **not say what any judge, lawyer, regulator, or other person will do**. For real disputes, get proper professional advice.

Each follows: principle vs ego → evidence vs emotion → proportionality → lower-conflict step → cost → the question to ask a professional.

---

## 1. Deciding whether to send an aggressive legal email

**Situation:** A supplier missed a deadline and I want to fire off a threatening "lawyer's coming" email.

- **Principle vs ego:** Some principle (a real contractual issue) but mostly anger and wanting them to feel it.
- **Evidence vs emotion:** Evidence = the contract terms, the dates, what was delivered. Emotion = "they disrespected me."
- **Proportionality:** A threat email is oversized for a first response to a missed deadline.
- **Lower-conflict step:** Organise the facts and ask a qualified professional what measured written step is appropriate before sending anything escalatory.
- **Cost:** A threat can end a workable relationship and box me in; a factual note keeps options open.
- **Question for a professional:** "Given this contract, what is a measured first written step that protects my position without escalating?"
- **Control / no control:** I control the tone and the record; I don't control their response.
- **Reminder:** Act to fix the problem, not to make them feel small.

## 2. Deciding whether to raise a court issue

**Situation:** I'm considering taking a dispute to court.

- **Principle vs ego:** Genuine question — is this about a real issue I should document and discuss with a qualified professional, or proving I'm right?
- **Evidence vs emotion:** Evidence = documents, records, written facts. Emotion = the story I've built about being wronged.
- **Proportionality:** Court is one of the largest, slowest, costliest responses. Is the harm that size?
- **Lower-conflict step:** Organise the facts and ask a qualified professional what proportionate options exist before escalating.
- **Cost:** Money, months of time, stress, focus, and strain on the relationship — win or lose.
- **Question for a professional:** "Based on my evidence, what are my realistic options short of court, and what would each require of me?" (Let the professional assess merits — this tool will not.)
- **Control / no control:** I control whether I prepare and seek advice; I don't control the process or any result.
- **Reminder:** Choose the smallest step that actually protects the real interest.

## 3. Deciding whether to respond to provocation

**Situation:** Someone is needling me publicly (a message, a comment, a post) and I want to hit back.

- **Principle vs ego:** Almost entirely ego — the bait is working.
- **Evidence vs emotion:** Evidence = nothing material is at stake. Emotion = humiliation, the urge to defend my image.
- **Proportionality:** Any heated response is oversized for bait.
- **Lower-conflict step:** Don't engage on their terms. If there's a real issue under it, address that privately and factually; otherwise, let it pass and document if it continues.
- **Cost:** Responding hands them a reaction and can create a record I'll regret.
- **Question for a professional:** "What records should I keep, and what should I do if this continues or becomes unsafe?"
- **Control / no control:** I control whether I take the bait; I don't control their behaviour.
- **Reminder:** Not every provocation deserves a reply.

## 4. Deciding whether to spend more money on a dispute

**Situation:** I've already spent a lot fighting this and I'm tempted to spend more.

- **Principle vs ego:** Watch for sunk cost — "I've come too far to stop" is ego and loss-aversion, not principle.
- **Evidence vs emotion:** Evidence = what the next spend actually buys me. Emotion = not wanting the past spend to be "wasted."
- **Proportionality:** Is this important enough to justify asking a qualified professional about the next step, judged forward and ignoring what's already gone?
- **Lower-conflict step:** Pause. Get a clear scope and cost for the next stage before committing, and define what would make me stop.
- **Cost:** More money, time, stress, and attention before I know whether the next step is worth taking.
- **Question for a professional:** "For the next stage only, what's the cost, what does it achieve, and what are my off-ramps?"
- **Control / no control:** I control the next dollar; I don't control the outcome or recover the past spend by spending more.
- **Reminder:** Decide forward from here, not backward from what's already gone.

## 5. Deciding whether the fight is about principle or ego

**Situation:** I keep saying "it's the principle," but I'm not sure anymore.

- **Principle vs ego — the test:** Would I still act this way if no one ever knew I'd "won," if I couldn't punish them, and if it cost me real time and money? If the energy drops when winning and punishing are removed, it's mostly ego.
- **Evidence vs emotion:** Write the case in two columns — verifiable facts vs feelings, assumptions, and interpretation. If the facts column is thin, slow down.
- **Proportionality:** Match the response to the facts column, not the feelings column.
- **Lower-conflict step:** State the outcome I want in one sentence, then separate that from whether a qualified professional thinks any action is appropriate.
- **Cost:** Principle is worth real cost; ego usually isn't worth what it asks.
- **Question for a professional:** "Here are the facts and the outcome I want — what proportionate steps fit, and is this worth pursuing?"
- **Control / no control:** I control my motive and my next step; I don't control vindication.
- **Reminder:** If removing winning and punishing kills the urge, it was ego.
